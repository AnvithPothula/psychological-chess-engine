# Game review: the first 20 games (to 2 October 2026)

Every move of every game the account has played, scored by Stockfish at depth
16 (`python -m src.eval.game_review`). 16 against bots, 4 against humans.
Per-game details, with every mistake by either side, are in the appendix.

## Record

16 wins (14 by mate, 2 by resignation), 4 losses, all 4 on time. **No game was
lost on the board.** The bot made no blunder (300cp or more) in any game and
missed no forced mate.

## The four losses: bugs, not chess (fixed in `e3809bc`)

In each, the bot was moving in 0.1-3.6s and then never moved again; the whole
remaining clock (about 5.5 minutes) ran out on one move.

| game | bot eval when it stopped | cause |
|---|---|---|
| [EPngOS1B](https://lichess.org/EPngOS1B) | -1.75 (worse) | a dropped connection while sending a move killed the game thread |
| [aevBdKIp](https://lichess.org/aevBdKIp) | **+7.84 (winning)** | same |
| [fUeLxEJr](https://lichess.org/fUeLxEJr) | **+7.92 (winning)** | the next move could repeat a third time; the bridge called that game over and waited |
| [AqpmpSQx](https://lichess.org/AqpmpSQx) | +0.56 (level) | same |

- **Dropped connection.** Lichess closed an idle keep-alive connection just as
  a move went out. berserk reports that as `ApiError`, which the retry code did
  not catch, so the game thread died. It is now retried, and a dropped game
  stream is rejoined instead of the game being abandoned.
- **Claimable repetition.** python-chess calls a draw "claimable" one move
  *before* the third repetition; Lichess does not end the game there. The
  bridge now waits only on positions that are over by the rules. The search
  already counts a real third repetition as a draw, so in fUeLxEJr it would
  have played on at +8. If the search ever declines a position, the bridge now
  plays Stockfish's best move instead of resigning, which is what it did before.
- All four losses were as Black at 5+3. That is coincidence of when the bugs
  fired, not a colour or time-control problem: the bot's clock was never low.

Two won games were thrown away. That is the most expensive finding here.

## How the bot plays

- **Accuracy is high.** Average centipawn loss per game: 4-38 (median 9).
  30 inaccuracies or worse in 20 games, none a blunder. 11 of the 30 happened in
  positions already won by more than +3 before and after; they cost nothing.
- **The other 19 are mostly deliberate.** Replaying the 17 that were not book
  moves with the live search setup (same proposer, Maia at the opponent's rating)
  picks the same move at every depth: Nd7, dxe5, Bb3, e5, Nfd2, Qxf6, Ba6, Be7.
  The search is giving up up to its 180cp safety floor for a move it predicts the
  opponent will mishandle. That is the design, not a search failure. Whether it
  pays against humans is exactly what this project is trying to measure; against
  bots it costs a little and still wins.
- **Some are noise.** The same search replayed on the same position does not
  always pick the same move (17...h6 live, 17...a4 on replay), because Stockfish's
  evaluations vary run to run by more than the margins between candidates.
- **Opening book costs.** Two of the opening errors were trap-book moves: 3.c3
  (Danish Gambit, -75) and 1...e5 (Englund Gambit, -118). The Englund game was the
  only game the bot was ever worse in, from move 1 to the end. Trap lines are
  aimed at humans who fall for them; against bots they are just bad openings.
- **Conversion is slow but safe.** From the first lasting +3 advantage to the end
  took 6-68 plies (median about 21). In [smN3r5ll](https://lichess.org/smN3r5ll) the bot
  was +6.3 from move 4 and mated on move 38, often not choosing Stockfish's
  fastest move. The evaluation still rose steadily, it never let a won game slip
  on the board, and against humans a longer game is more chances to blunder.

## Time management

| clock | mean think per move | longest |
|---|---|---|
| 3+0 | 0.2-1.0s | 3.5s |
| 3+2 | 0.6-1.4s | 3.8s |
| 5+0 | 0.6-1.2s | 1.5s |
| 5+3 | 1.4s | 5.2s |
| 10+5 | 1.3-1.4s | 5.2s |

With an increment the bot thinks for about half the increment, so its clock only
ever grows; in 3+0 it finished with 162-164s of 180 left. It is never in time
trouble. That looks like wasted time, so I tested spending it: the 17 costly
positions replayed at deeper search profiles.

| profile | mean loss | errors of 100cp+ | time per move |
|---|---|---|---|
| current deepest (depth 10/12) | 48.8cp | 1 | 2.4s |
| deep (12/14) | 73.9cp | 6 | 5.8s |
| deeper (14/16) | 58.4cp | 3 | 20.4s |

**Deeper search does not play better here**, because the costly moves are the
objective choosing them, not the search failing to see further. No change.

## Opponents

Opponents averaged 15-93 centipawns lost per move, and made 13 blunders in total,
10 of them by bots. Bot blunders say little about the psychological search,
which models human error; they are engine weaknesses. These games are for rating
and visibility, not evidence.

## Recommendations, in order

1. **Done:** the two timeout bugs (`e3809bc`). They were the only losses.
2. **Consider:** skip the trap book against bot opponents. It cost the only bad
   game and has nothing to exploit in an engine. Not done; it changes the arms.
3. **Leave alone:** time management. Unused clock is real, but deeper search did
   not reduce the costly moves.
4. **Watch:** conversion speed in won positions. Harmless so far.
