## Four losses on time: the Mac went to sleep (fixed)

In each, the bot was moving normally and then froze. Its clock was 108-621s, and
it was never in time trouble. The cause is in macOS's power log (`pmset -g log`):

| game | started | the Mac |
|---|---|---|
| [LT2peZoj](https://lichess.org/LT2peZoj) | 20:15 | slept at 20:17:01 |
| [6Trmyhzk](https://lichess.org/6Trmyhzk) | 23:49 | slept at 23:53:41, then woke and slept repeatedly: one move took 587s, then the bot froze again |
| [mbrdHNRv](https://lichess.org/mbrdHNRv) | 05:47 | was in a brief maintenance wake; the challenger started a game, and the Mac slept 15s later |
| [R9TrezZN](https://lichess.org/R9TrezZN) | 08:57 | same overnight sleep cycling |

The pipelines always ran under `caffeinate`; the bot never did. It now holds
`caffeinate -i -s` for its own lifetime, so idle sleep is blocked, and system
sleep on AC power, however it is started. **Closing the lid still sleeps the
Mac**, so the bot needs the lid open or an external display.

The bot's log also existed only in the terminal, and that scrollback was cleared
before this review, which is why the diagnosis came from the power log. It now
also writes `build/bot.log`, rotated at 10MB with three backups.

## Eight losses on the board: stronger opponents, not bugs

All eight were against engines rated 2063-2417. The bot was never better than
+3.3 in any of them, its clock never dropped below its starting time, and most
were long (68-219 plies). The challenger asks bots within 300 points of the bot's
rating, which rose to 2280, so 2300-2400 engines are now routine opponents. With
the human profile's depth cap, a 2400 engine is simply stronger. This is the "not
too OP" result asked for.
