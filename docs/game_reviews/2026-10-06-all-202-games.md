## The eleventh loss on time: a stream that stops sending moves (fixed in 0d4233f)

In [KYmceMVK](https://lichess.org/KYmceMVK) the bot played 66...Rb1+ and then
received nothing for exactly ten minutes, until the server reset the
connection with the bot's clock at zero. The 20-second read timeout from the
earlier fix did not fire. The same log shows it firing on the bot's other
connection, so it works; here the connection was never quiet. Lichess kept
sending keep-alives but no game updates. The two earlier ten-minute stalls
(tq8SjaTx, wczLemzW) had the same signature, so the read timeout was aimed at
the wrong failure for them.

The game stream is now read on its own thread. After 15 seconds of silence the
bot asks Lichess's ongoing-games endpoint whether it owes a move. It asks only
while waiting, never while searching, so a move owed is a move the stream failed
to deliver, and the bot reconnects.

All eleven time losses now have a known, fixed cause:

| cause | games | fixed in |
|---|---|---|
| a dropped connection killed the game thread | 2 | e3809bc |
| a claimable-but-not-yet repetition treated as game over | 2 | e3809bc |
| a stream that stays open but stops sending moves | 3 | 0d4233f |
| the Mac went to sleep mid-game | 4 | 92f5253 |

## Where lost and drawn games are decided

The late, ugly moves in long losses (100.Bf7, 54.Kf1, 52...b2) were played in
positions already lost. The endgame is where the bot plays best:

| phase | bot moves | mean loss | errors of 100cp+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20+ pieces | 4,424 | 17.9cp | 4.0% |
| 13-19 pieces | 2,251 | 16.5cp | 4.1% |
| 7-12 pieces | 2,575 | 10.8cp | 2.4% |
| 6 or fewer | 1,495 | 4.9cp | 1.0% |

In 8 of the 13 board losses, and 19 of the 38 draws, the bot's costliest move
came with 20 or more pieces on the board. That is where the expectimax trades
objective accuracy for moves the human model predicts the opponent will
mishandle, and the opponents are engines.

## Smaller finding

In [9jiA6YfJ](https://lichess.org/9jiA6YfJ) the bot's move crossed the server's
threefold draw and was rejected, and the bridge dropped the game without reading
its result. It now keeps listening after a rejected move (commit after 0d4233f).
The same case shows Lichess does end a game itself once a position has actually
repeated three times.
